One filter, the toolbar at the bottom, and a screen before Claude acts

A desktop window and a phone (393 by 852, an iPhone 16) side by side. Both work. Nothing here is built yet.

Try it: press the filter in the toolbar, then Claude ▸ Revise (or Build) in the header

Desktop

Phone

1One filter: what still needs you

  • The toolbar keeps one toggle, the funnel. On, the book shows only what still needs you: every line not accepted yet (changed or new) and every line with a note. Sections with none hide; the rest of a shown section is dimmed. Off, the whole book.
  • The arrows step through those same lines, on or off, so 1 / 6 counts what needs you.
  • The Requests button and its panel go: notes stay where you dropped them in the book, and the full list is on the screen before Revise (below).
  • F still toggles the filter.

2A screen before Claude acts

  • Revise no longer sends at once. It opens a screen with every note that will go to Claude, grouped by section: the requirement it is on (its id and text, marked if it changed), then the note under it, and who wrote it when it was not you. Go to › takes you to that spot in the book. Send to Claude sends them; Cancel goes back to the book. On a desktop it is a dialog over the book; on a phone a sheet from the bottom, its buttons in reach of the thumb.
  • Build gets the same: the sections marked Ready that it will build, then Build or Cancel.
  • The Claude button carries the notes count, and so does Revise in its menu, so you see notes waiting without opening anything.
  • Show what Claude receives opens the words themselves, as you would paste them into Claude, with Copy: each note with where it is, the requirement it is on, and the note, and a collaborator's note marked as theirs. To make it true, the request would carry this text and the watch would hand it to Claude as it is, so what you read here is exactly what Claude gets.

3The toolbar at the bottom everywhere, and a header with less in it

  • The review toolbar (Contents, Comment, previous and next, the filter) sits at the bottom on a desktop too: floating, centered under the book. On a phone it is the bottom strip, as today.
  • The header drops Contents ▾ and the review buttons. It keeps the book's title, View, Share and Claude.
  • On a desktop, the toolbar's Contents button hides and shows the contents sidebar (open by default; hidden, the book takes the whole width). On a phone it is a plain button that opens the contents as a sheet from the bottom, as tall as the screen allows (a strip of the book stays in view above it), scrolling inside; choosing a section or tapping outside closes it.
  • Every dropdown arrow is the toolbar's chevron: 18 pixels, the same stroke, in the button's own color.
